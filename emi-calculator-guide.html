What is an EMI Calculator?

An EMI Calculator is an online tool that helps you estimate the Equated Monthly Instalment for a loan. By entering the loan amount, interest rate and loan tenure, you can estimate your monthly payment and understand the overall repayment amount.

EMI calculations can be useful when comparing different loan amounts and repayment periods. This can help you understand how changing the tenure or interest rate may affect your monthly payment.

How is EMI Calculated?

For a standard reducing-balance loan, a commonly used EMI formula is:

EMI = P × R × (1 + R)N ÷ ((1 + R)N − 1)
  • P = Principal loan amount
  • R = Monthly interest rate
  • N = Total number of monthly instalments

If the annual interest rate is given, it is generally converted into a monthly rate before applying the formula.

Example of EMI Calculation

Example:
Loan amount = ₹5,00,000
Annual interest rate = 10%
Loan tenure = 5 years

The EMI Calculator can use these three values to estimate the monthly instalment. It can also help estimate the total amount paid over the complete loan tenure and the interest component.

The actual repayment amount may differ because lenders can apply different terms, fees, charges or interest calculation methods.

Factors That Affect Your EMI

1. Loan Amount

A higher principal amount generally results in a higher EMI when other factors remain unchanged.

2. Interest Rate

The interest rate affects both the monthly instalment and the overall interest paid during the loan period.

3. Loan Tenure

A longer tenure can reduce the monthly EMI, but it may increase the total interest paid over the entire repayment period.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment. It is the regular monthly payment made towards a loan according to the applicable repayment terms.

How is EMI calculated?

For a standard reducing-balance loan, EMI can be calculated using principal, monthly interest rate and the number of monthly instalments.

What information is needed to calculate EMI?

You generally need the loan amount, annual interest rate and loan tenure.

Is the calculated EMI always the exact bank EMI?

No. It is an estimate. Actual EMI and repayment can vary according to lender terms, fees, charges and interest calculation methods.
